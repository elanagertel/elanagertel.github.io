In a scientific illustration course, I chose the sabra. Israel is my only home, but I'm not called a "tzabar", because I was born in America. Then I found out the sabra came from America too. So why did an immigrant plant become the symbol of the rooted Israeli?

The sabra spreads fast, roots deep and survives with little water. A tzabar isn't someone born here, but someone who puts down roots and survives the hardship. Suddenly I was the most tzabar of all.

I chose the David font, spiky and round like the cactus, and colored pencil on black: vivid color with a dry feel, where hardship meets vitality. Watercolor was too romantic, pen too clean, chalk too forced.

The result is a booklet, read slowly, page after page. Readers got goosebumps, and some thought of grandparents who always felt like immigrants. Next time, I'd let the illustrations break out of the grid, less obedient, like the sabra.
